## Design Purpose

HelpMate was designed to solve a problem that is quietly felt by thousands of students and new residents in Melbourne every day: **living surrounded by people, yet having no one nearby to turn to for help.**

Our goal was to create a platform that lowers the social and practical barriers to asking for and offering help at a hyper-local level — within the same building, street, or suburb. Rather than a transactional service, HelpMate is designed to **spark genuine community connections**, using a map-based interface, a mutual trust and reputation system, and a gamified engagement layer that makes helping others feel rewarding and fun.

The design is grounded in real user research, from interviews, surveys, and structured design frameworks, rather than assumptions. Every major feature decision traces back to something a real participant told us.

---

## The Problem

Through our investigation, we identified three interconnected problems:

### 1. Social Isolation in Dense Urban Settings
Despite living in densely populated areas, most of our participants had **never spoken to their immediate neighbours** — even after six months to a year of living there. Proximity does not automatically create community.

### 2. No Reliable Local Help Network
When participants needed small everyday help, like borrowing a screwdriver, moving furniture, storing food during a fridge breakdown, dealing with a spider at 11pm — their only options were:
- Bother a friend who lives far away
- Buy something they'd use only once
- Give up entirely

Existing platforms (Facebook groups, Nextdoor) were found to be **slow, impersonal, full of scams, and not designed for immediate local exchange.**

### 3. Safety and Trust Barriers
Female participants in particular raised significant concerns about **inviting unverified strangers into their home**. The lack of any identity verification or accountability on existing platforms was a key reason they avoided seeking help at all.

---

## Target Users

HelpMate is primarily designed for:

- **International students** at Australian universities — new to the country, with small social networks and a strong need for local support
- **New residents and recent arrivals** in Melbourne's urban and suburban areas — unfamiliar with the community around them
- **Working Holiday visa holders** — living independently, often in shared housing, with limited access to support networks

**Secondary users:**
- Longer-term residents who want to contribute to their local community and build reputation as trusted helpers

**Key characteristics of our target users:**
- Age range: approximately 22–35
- Living situation: apartment buildings, flat-shares, student accommodation
- Often describe themselves as introverted or hesitant to ask for help
- Motivated by social connection, not just task completion

## Investigation Methods

Our research followed a structured, multi-method approach to ensure every design decision was grounded in real user evidence.

### Semi-Structured Interviews

We conducted **9 semi-structured interviews** with international students and new residents living in Melbourne, each lasting 20–30 minutes. Interviews were conducted in person at locations convenient to participants, including student accommodation common areas and on-campus spaces.

**Participant profile:**
- 5 female, 4 male
- Age range: approximately 22–28
- All postgraduate students or recent graduates at the University of Melbourne
- Cultural backgrounds: East and Southeast Asian (Taiwan, China, Hong Kong, Malaysia)
- Living situations: student accommodation, flat-shares, private apartment rentals
- Length of residence in Melbourne: 1 month to 3.5 years

**Interview structure (6 sections):**
1. Background and living situation
2. Current help-seeking behaviours
3. Challenges and pain points with existing solutions
4. Reactions to the HelpMate concept
5. Trust, safety, and identity preferences
6. Feature priorities

All interviews were audio-recorded with participant consent, transcribed, and analysed using a systematic **coding process** taught in the course workshop. Codes were grouped into themes through **thematic analysis**, and the resulting themes were used to construct the affinity diagram.

**Key quotes from participants:**

> *"Melbourne is like a country jungle — everyone has different lifestyles and we are both unfamiliar with each other."*
> — Female participant, Docklands, 1 year in Melbourne

> *"Most of my close friends are not in the same country, so they cannot help me immediately."*
> — Male participant, Master of Marketing Communication

> *"My biggest concern is not safety — it's that there are not enough users."*
> — Female participant, South Yarra, Working Holiday

> *"If there is a man who responds and I'm living alone, I might be worried about what happens if that man comes inside."*
> — Female participant, South Yarra

### Online Survey

To extend our research beyond the 9 interviews, we developed and distributed an **online survey** via the University of Melbourne's Qualtrics platform, reaching a broader and more diverse group of respondents across Melbourne.

The survey covered 5 sections:
1. **Background** — living situation, neighbourhood familiarity, safety experiences
2. **Behaviours** — help-seeking and help-giving patterns, platform usage
3. **Mindset** — expected features and design preferences
4. **Attitudes toward gamification** — Likert-scale ratings of game-inspired features
5. **Feedback** — open and multiple-choice reactions to the HelpMate concept

Respondents represented a wide cross-section of Melbourne's urban community — domestic and international students, graduates, working professionals, and residents across a range of ages, cultural backgrounds, and living situations. The survey confirmed that core themes from interviews — social isolation, safety concerns, interest in gamification, and frustration with existing platforms — were widely shared beyond our immediate interview cohort.

### Affinity Diagram

After completing the thematic analysis of interview transcripts, the team built an **affinity diagram** to visually organise and consolidate all coded insights. Individual codes and observations were grouped spatially by theme, allowing the team to:

- See relationships between ideas across participants
- Identify gaps in the research
- Prioritise the most significant findings
- Directly discuss and decide **which features must be included in HelpMate**

The affinity diagram served as the bridge between raw research data and concrete design decisions — tracing a clear line from participant pain points to product requirements.

### Value Proposition Canvas

Using the **Value Proposition Canvas** framework (taught in the course workshop), the team mapped the relationship between what users need and what HelpMate delivers:

**Customer Profile:**
- **Functional Jobs** — get urgent help nearby quickly, find someone with a specific skill, offer help in exchange for recognition
- **Social Jobs** — build a visible local reputation, connect with peers, make friends in a new community, belong to a trusted network
- **Emotional Jobs** — feel safe meeting a stranger for a task, reduce anxiety about asking for help, feel valued for helping
- **Pains** — no fast way to find nearby help, fear of unverified strangers, ghosting and no-shows, social embarrassment of asking, fragmented platforms
- **Gains** — find help within minutes, visible and portable reputation, feel safe throughout the exchange, build real friendships through exchanges

**Value Map:**
- **Products & Services** — map interface, post system, chat, identity verification, XP system, leaderboard, avatar, mini-game
- **Pain Relievers** — mandatory identity verification, gender filters, dual-confirmation lifecycle, structured offer cards, mutual review system
- **Gain Creators** — XP tiers and badges, leaderboard visibility, friend system, pre-exchange checklists, transparent trust scores

## Key Features

### Map-Based Help Discovery
A live map shows nearby help requests and supply offers as colour-coded markers:
- **Red** — Need (requesting help)
- **Green** — Supply (offering help)
- **Blue** — Friend posts

Users can tap any marker to see post details and contact the person. A **list view** is also available for browsing without the map. Filters can be toggled directly on the map by tapping the **Need / Supply / Friend** pills below the search bar.

### Post Requests and Offers
Users can post either a **help request** (something they need) or a **help offer** (something they can provide), with category tagging, urgency flags, availability times, and optional photos.

### Smart Filtering
Filter map markers by:
- **Post type** (Need / Supply / Friend) — tap directly on the map
- **Distance radius** (0.5 km, 1 km, 2 km, 5 km)
- **Gender preference** (for safety-sensitive situations)
- **Search keyword**

### Trust and Safety System
- **Identity verification** at registration
- **Mutual review and rating system** — both the helper and the help-seeker are reviewed after every completed task
- **Public trust score** visible on every profile, built from verified completed exchanges
- **Visible exchange history** so users can assess someone's track record before agreeing to meet
- **Block and report** function for misconduct

### Gamification and Engagement
- **XP progression system** with named tiers (Newcomer → Helper → Contributor → Champion)
- **Skill-specific badges** for completing exchanges in particular categories (e.g. "Reliable Driver", "Tutor Pro")
- **Weekly leaderboard** highlighting the most active helpers in a local area
- **Avatar customisation** — users create and personalise a 3D avatar using Tripo AI
- **Mini-game** for additional engagement beyond task exchanges

### Friend and Community System
- Add friends from people you have exchanged with
- Receive notifications when friends post new requests
- Build a visible local social network over time

### In-App Communication
- Secure in-app chat between requester and helper
- Structured offer card system (helpers send a formal response before meeting)
- Pre-exchange checklist so both parties confirm details before meeting in person
- Messaging privacy controls (friends only or everyone)

The HelpMate mobile app is built with React Native + Expo and connects to a Supabase backend. See the [HelpMate app README](./HelpMate/README.md) for full installation and setup instructions.

**Quick start for testers (Expo Go — no Xcode or cable required):**
```bash

cd HelpMate
npm install
npx expo start 
```
